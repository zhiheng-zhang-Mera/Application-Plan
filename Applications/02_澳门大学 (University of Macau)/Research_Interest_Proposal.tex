\textbf{Provisional title:} Trustworthy Spatiotemporal Learning for Dynamic Urban Decision Systems

\section*{Research Interest and Motivation}
Urban, mobility, and networked data change across locations, populations, sensors, and time. Models trained on convenient historical snapshots can fail when spatial coverage shifts, observations arrive late, or context changes. The proposed research asks how spatiotemporal learning systems can remain temporally valid, privacy-aware, and operationally useful under these changes.

Quant-Ultra supplies an implemented starting point rather than proof of novelty: it connects point-in-time data, controlled evaluation, constrained decisions, monitoring, reconciliation, and evidence bundles. Privacy Lens supplies a second setting for provenance and fail-closed behavior. The doctoral contribution would be a school-specific research program with verified literature, defensible baselines, and supervisor-guided refinement.

\section*{Research Questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item How can spatiotemporal data contracts represent observation time, location context, revisions, missingness, and permitted feature transformations?
\item Which online or graph-learning methods remain calibrated when spatial coverage and temporal regimes change together?
\item How should privacy, latency, resource, and decision constraints be incorporated without hiding failures behind a single aggregate score?
\item What provenance and monitoring evidence is sufficient to reproduce and challenge an urban decision result?
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Proposed Methodology}
\textbf{Spatiotemporal benchmark.} Construct versioned public and synthetic mobility or network datasets with controlled spatial gaps, delayed observations, revisions, and regime changes.

\textbf{Adaptive learning.} Compare temporal, graph, and online-learning baselines using location-held-out and rolling-window evaluation with fold-local preprocessing.

\textbf{Constrained decisions.} Evaluate calibrated predictions through separately auditable decision rules with privacy, latency, resource, and robustness constraints.

\textbf{Distributed evidence.} Prototype provenance-aware pipelines that preserve data lineage, geographic coverage, model versions, monitoring results, and failure states.

\section*{Expected Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A benchmark for coupled spatial and temporal distribution shift.
\item Evaluation protocols that separate predictive accuracy, geographic robustness, privacy, and decision utility.
\item A provenance-aware architecture for distributed spatiotemporal learning.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Preparation, Feasibility, and Risks}
The existing systems support early prototyping but do not establish research novelty, investment validity, legal compliance, or publication. Doctoral work requires a verified literature review, ethical and licensing review, simple baselines, preregistered ablations where appropriate, and independent replication. Negative or unstable results will be retained rather than filtered from the evidence record.

Data licensing may restrict redistribution, so experiments will combine redistributable sources, synthetic controls, and published transformation code. System complexity may obscure causal conclusions; modular experiments and failure injection will keep individual mechanisms testable.

\section*{Indicative Plan}
Year 1: literature review and benchmark design. Year 2: adaptive spatiotemporal learning and calibration. Year 3: constrained decision and distributed provenance experiments. Final period: cross-dataset validation and dissertation integration.
